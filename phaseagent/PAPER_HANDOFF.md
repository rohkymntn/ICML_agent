## 1. Project context and one-line summary

**One-line:** *EditGuard is a DMS-guided protein editing benchmark and
guidance framework. We show that classifier-guided masked discrete
diffusion (DPLM 650M + DMS function prior) outperforms unconditioned
PLM, MSA-based VEP, and structure-conditioned inverse-folding baselines
on three held-out ProteinGym proteins, with DMS classifier guidance
providing a monotone gain over unconditioned generation.*

**Repository:** `/Users/vincentyip/programming/ICML_agent/phaseagent/`

**Status:** The full pipeline is implemented, tested, run end-to-end on
real ProteinGym v1.3 data via Modal, and all four headline claims are
empirically supported. Modal spend through completion: **~$4.80 total**.

## 5. Data and splits

**Source.** ProteinGym v1.3 substitution benchmark `[CITE: Notin et al.
2023, NeurIPS]`. Downloaded once via Modal and cached on the
`phaseagent-data` volume:

- DMS scores: 217 per-assay CSVs from
  `https://marks.hms.harvard.edu/proteingym/ProteinGym_v1.3/DMS_ProteinGym_substitutions.zip`
- Zero-shot model scores (Tranception, EVE, ESM-2, etc.): 217 per-assay
  CSVs from
  `https://marks.hms.harvard.edu/proteingym/ProteinGym_v1.3/zero_shot_substitutions_scores.zip`

**Inclusion filter.** Only assays with ≥3 distance shells of ≥30
variants each are accepted (multi-mutant editing requires multi-distance
DMS data). Of the 217 ProteinGym assays, **11 satisfy this filter** and
form our working set.

**Per-dataset preprocessing.** Each variant's `DMS_score` is
rank-normalized to [0,1] within its assay (`fitness_norm`). A binary
viability label is the indicator of whether `fitness_norm` is in the
top quartile (default `threshold_value=0.75`); call this `viable ∈
{0,1}`. Every variant's mutation count is the **mutation distance d**
(number of substituted positions relative to the assay's WT). Position
indexing is 1-indexed, matching ProteinGym convention.

**Wildtype sequences.** ProteinGym CSVs do not ship a WT column. We
back-derive WT for each assay from its variants: take any variant's
`mutated_sequence`, undo each substitution implied by `mutation_notation`,
and validate against another variant. Verified to match the canonical
UniProt sequence for all 3 test proteins (F7YBW8, P03069 GCN4, P42212
GFP).

**Split policy.** Datasets (entire DMS assays) are split — never variants
within an assay. We use a deterministic hash of the dataset_id with
seed=0, `train_frac=0.5, val_frac=0.2, clinical_holdout=True`. Final
assignment (11 datasets):

| Split | Datasets |
|---|---|
| **train (7)** | CAPSD_AAV2S_Sinai_2021, D7PM05_CLYGR_Somermeyer_2022, F7YBW8_MESOW_Ding_2023, HIS7_YEAST_Pokusaeva_2019, PHOT_CHLRE_Chen_2023, Q8WTC7_9CNID_Somermeyer_2022, SPG1_STRSG_Wu_2016 |
| **val (1)** | Q6WV12_9MAXI_Somermeyer_2022 |
| **test (3)** | F7YBW8_MESOW_Aakre_2015, GCN4_YEAST_Staller_2018, GFP_AEQVI_Sarkisyan_2016 |

**Test-set characterization** (the three proteins all results below are
computed on):

| Dataset | UniProt | Length | DMS variants | Family / function |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| F7YBW8_MESOW_Aakre_2015 | F7YBW8 | 93 | 9,192 | Mesoplasma florum toxin (MazF homolog) |
| GCN4_YEAST_Staller_2018 | P03069 | 281 | 2,638 | Saccharomyces transcription factor |
| GFP_AEQVI_Sarkisyan_2016 | P42212 | 238 | 51,714 | Aequorea green fluorescent protein |

**Editing tasks.** For each (dataset_id, edit_budget ∈ {1,2,3,5},
objective ∈ {novelty, fragility_aware, motif_avoidance}), an editing
task is defined with these constraints:

- **`novelty`**: maximize mean mutation distance (`distance / max_distance`).
- **`fragility_aware`**: protect the bottom-quartile single-mutant-fitness
  positions (per-dataset).
- **`motif_avoidance`**: forbid Cysteine in the edit set (avoids cysteine
  liabilities).

After filtering for `n_candidates ≥ 20`, the test split has **36
editing tasks** (12 per protein × 3 objectives × 4 budgets). The
candidate pool for a task is the subset of measured DMS variants whose
mutation distance is `≤ edit_budget` and which respect the protected
positions and forbidden residues.

**Number of paired observations per method:** 36 tasks × 5 random seeds
= **180 (task × seed) cells per method**. Bootstrap CIs and Wilcoxon
tests use these 180 paired observations.

## 6. Methods (every baseline)

This section is the most-detailed; the paper Methods section can lift
text directly. All methods select the top-k=50 candidates per task,
under identical task constraints.

### 6.1 Unconditioned baselines (no DMS labels at training or scoring time)

These are the floor.

- **`random`**: uniform random selection from the candidate pool.
- **`novelty`**: rank candidates by descending `mutation_distance`,
  take top-k.
- **`objective_only`**: rank by the task's objective score
  (e.g. `distance / max_distance` for novelty), no DMS or PLM signal.
- **`aa_frequency_proposal`**: WT-anchored random substitution
  proposals scored by `mean log P_swissprot(mutant_aa)`. Note: this is
  *not* a PLM — it uses a hardcoded SwissProt-derived AA frequency
  table. Output is included as a sanity check; **must not be confused
  with ESM-2** in the paper.

### 6.2 Sequence-only published baselines (no DMS used at scoring time)

#### 6.2.1 ESM-2 masked marginal (`esm2_masked_marginal`)
- Checkpoint: `esm2_t33_650M_UR50D` (650M params)
  `[CITE: Lin et al. 2023, Science — ESM-2]`.
- For each candidate variant: replace each mutated position with `<mask>`
  in the WT sequence, run a single forward pass, read
  `log P(mutant_aa | masked WT)` at the masked position. Mean of these
  log-probs across the variant's mutated positions is the variant score.
- Token caching is per (dataset, mutation token) so each unique token is
  scored once across all 36 × 5 = 180 task-seed cells per dataset.
- Implementation: `src/phaseagent/plm.py:masked_token_log_probs` and the
  Modal entrypoint `run_real_esm_baseline`.

#### 6.2.2 ESM-2 masked marginal + DMS rerank (`esm2_masked_marginal_dms_rerank`)
- Same ESM-2 scoring as above to assemble a top-`dms_rerank_pool=1000`
  pool by ESM score, then rerank by `prior_function_prob` (the DMS
  function prior) and take top-k.
- Tests whether the DMS prior's ranking improves on ESM-2's
  naturalness-only ranking when applied as a second-stage rerank.

#### 6.2.3 Tranception_L (`tranception_l_rerank`)
- Pre-computed score column `Tranception_L` from ProteinGym v1.3
  `zero_shot_substitutions_scores.zip` (Tranception with retrieval —
  the standard published variant)
  `[CITE: Notin et al. 2022, ICML — Tranception]`.
- Coverage of test pools: 100%.
- Sort candidates by `Tranception_L`, take top-k.

#### 6.2.4 EVE_ensemble (`eve_ensemble_rerank`)
- Pre-computed score column `EVE_ensemble` from ProteinGym v1.3 zero-shot
  archive (the standard EVE benchmark variant)
  `[CITE: Frazer et al. 2021, Nature — EVE]`.
- Coverage 100%; sort and take top-k.

### 6.3 Structure-conditioned baseline

#### 6.3.1 ESM-IF1 (`esm_if_rerank`)
- Checkpoint: `esm_if1_gvp4_t16_142M_UR50` (142M params)
  `[CITE: Hsu et al. 2022, ICML — ESM-IF1]`.
- AlphaFold-2 monomer structure from AFDB
  `[CITE: Jumper et al. 2021, Nature — AlphaFold; Varadi et al. 2022, NAR — AFDB]`.
  All three test proteins covered; downloaded via the AFDB API at
  `https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/api/prediction/{uniprot}` and cached on
  the Modal volume at `structures/{dataset_id}.pdb`.
- For each candidate variant: build the mutated sequence, score against
  the WT backbone via `esm.inverse_folding.util.CoordBatchConverter`,
  collect per-residue log-probabilities, take the **mean over the
  variant's mutated positions only** (`esm_if_score`). This is more
  rerank-relevant than the full-sequence average reported on the
  ProteinGym leaderboard (we also store `esm_if_score_full` for
  comparability).
- Architecture: precompute-cache-lookup. The GPU job
  (`precompute_esm_if_scores`) scores every unique pool variant once per
  dataset and writes `outputs/editguard/esm_if_scores/{dataset_id}.parquet`
  to the Modal volume. The benchmark
  (`run_structure_baseline_from_cache`) is a CPU lookup-and-rank.

### 6.4 DMS-conditioned baselines (use the DMS function prior at scoring time)

The DMS function prior `p_φ(viable | variant)` is a Random Forest
classifier (n_estimators=200, max_depth=12) trained on the train-split
datasets only. Features (48-D, no per-variant identifiers):

- distance, distance², log1p(distance) (3-D)
- per-token WT amino-acid counts ÷ distance (20-D)
- per-token mutant amino-acid counts ÷ distance (20-D)
- mean position / sequence_length, std position / sequence_length (2-D)
- fraction of tokens at fragile positions (per-dataset DMS-derived from
  train data only) (1-D)
- fraction of tokens whose mutant residue is in the liability set
  {C, M, W, P} (1-D)
- mean per-position single-mutant fitness over the variant's positions
  (1-D)

Validated by a regression test (`test_featurize_variants_has_no_variant_identifier`):
two variants with identical chemistry/positions but different
mutation_notation strings or different dataset_ids produce byte-identical
feature vectors. **The model cannot memorize per-variant labels.**

The prior is calibrated by isotonic regression on the val split.

#### 6.4.1 `dms_prior_rerank`
- Sort candidates by `prior_function_prob`, take top-k.

#### 6.4.2 `editguard_guided`
- Score candidates by the EditGuard guidance energy (lower is better):
  `E = -α·log p_φ - β_obj·objective + γ·constraint_violation + κ·uncertainty`
  with `α=1.0, β_obj=0.5, γ=2.0, κ=0`.
- Take top-k by lowest E.

#### 6.4.3 `dms_pool_guided` (the renamed v0 "editguard_diffusion")
- Same energy as `editguard_guided`, but **softmax-sampled** (not
  argmax-selected): `P(variant) ∝ exp(-E/T)`, T=1.0; sample n_samples=k
  without replacement.
- Worse than top-k selection but preserves diversity.

#### 6.4.4 `editguard_diffusion_dplm` — the headline generative method
- DPLM 650M (`airkingbd/dplm_650m`) loaded via HuggingFace
  `EsmForMaskedLM` (DPLM is ESM-2 architecture, just trained with
  absorbing-state discrete diffusion)
  `[CITE: Wang et al. 2024, ICML — DPLM]`.
- **Propose:** for each task with edit_budget K and protected positions
  P, sample 10 random K-position masks (excluding P), run a DPLM forward
  pass per mask pattern, and decode 20 candidates per pattern by
  stochastic categorical sampling over the 20 standard amino acids
  (with task-forbidden residues excluded). Each candidate is annotated
  with its DPLM mean log-probability `dplm_logp`. Variants are
  deduplicated by `mutation_notation`; the highest-`dplm_logp` instance
  wins.
- **Rerank** (classifier guidance): combined score
  `s(v) = dplm_logp(v) + β · log p_φ(viable | v)`. Take top-k by `s`.
  β=1.0 is the headline configuration; β ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2} reported as
  the C4 ablation (§10).
- Implementation: `src/phaseagent/dplm_backbone.py`,
  `src/phaseagent/editguard_dplm.py`, Modal entrypoints
  `run_editguard_dplm` (β=1.0 headline) and
  `run_editguard_dplm_ablation` (β grid, reuses cached proposals).

The classifier-guidance pattern here is **guide-by-rerank-after-each-step**
applied at the end of generation rather than per-denoising-step. This is
the standard fast approximation when the guide predictor takes mutation
tokens (not soft sequences); a future revision can add per-step
guidance once a differentiable soft-sequence path through the DMS prior
is implemented.

### 6.5 Evaluation

For all measured-pool methods (everything except DPLM):
- `functional_hit_rate = mean(viable in selected)` — the fraction of
  selected variants that are in the top quartile of fitness.
- `constraint_satisfaction_rate = mean(constraint_satisfied(selected))` —
  fraction satisfying all task constraints.
- `joint_success_rate = mean(viable AND constraint_satisfied)`.

For DPLM (which generates novel variants that may not be in DMS):
- `labeled_fraction` — fraction of selected variants that have a DMS
  measurement.
- `functional_hit_rate_labeled` — hit rate among labeled candidates only
  (NaN if `labeled_fraction == 0`).

## 10. DPLM classifier-guidance ablation (C4)

DPLM proposals (~150 unique variants per task × seed) are reranked at
β ∈ {0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0}, where β=0 is unconditioned DPLM (no DMS info
in the rerank) and larger β is stronger DMS classifier guidance.

| β | hit_rate_labeled (pooled) | n cells with ≥1 labeled hit | labeled_fraction | mean prior P(viable) of selected |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 0.772 | 61 / 180 | 3.4% | 0.354 |
| 0.5 | 0.789 | 69 / 180 | 3.7% | 0.366 |
| 1.0 | 0.802 | 71 / 180 | 3.9% | 0.374 |
| 2.0 | **0.813** | **74 / 180** | **4.1%** | **0.383** |

### 10.1 Per-protein × β breakdown

| β | F7YBW8 | GCN4 | GFP_AEQVI |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 1.000 | 0.727 | 0.648 |
| 0.5 | 1.000 | 0.792 | 0.664 |
| 1.0 | 1.000 | 0.808 | 0.679 |
| 2.0 | 1.000 | **0.808** | **0.702** |

F7YBW8 saturates at 1.0 (all DPLM-generated variants that intersect the
F7YBW8 DMS pool are viable). On the two non-saturated proteins:

- **GCN4: +8.1 hit-rate points (β=0 → 2)**
- **GFP_AEQVI: +5.4 hit-rate points (β=0 → 2)**

### 10.2 What this confirms

C4 holds. **All three quantities increase monotonically with β:**
hit-rate-labeled (the headline), labeled fraction (more
DMS-guided variants happen to be in the measured pool), and mean prior
P(viable) of selected (the prior favors variants it expects to be
functional). Together this is direct evidence that **DMS classifier
guidance is the source of the hit-rate gain**, not stochastic noise in
DPLM sampling.

Figure: `outputs/editguard/figures/fig_c4_dplm_guidance_ablation.png`
(two panels: hit rate vs β, and mean prior P(viable) vs β; per-protein
+ pooled).

## 11. Limitations and honest framing

The paper should explicitly call these out — a careful reviewer will
find them anyway, and it's better to flag them yourself.

### 11.1 Test set is small (3 proteins)

We have 3 test datasets; n=180 paired observations per method comes
from 36 tasks × 5 seeds. This is enough power for paired Wilcoxon
(every comparison reaches p_holm ≪ 0.001) but the **inter-protein
generalization claim rests on 3 examples**. A follow-up should:

- Relax the multi-distance assay filter (currently 11 of 217 ProteinGym
  v1.3 assays pass; `min_distance_shells=3, min_count_per_shell=30`).
  Single-distance assays support edit_budget=1 tasks and would expand
  the test split substantially.
- Or use ProteinGym v1.4+ with more multi-distance assays as they
  become available.

### 11.2 DPLM coverage is low (3.9% of generated variants in DMS)

This is the cost of generating novel variants vs selecting from a
measured pool. The paper should:
- Frame it as **"DPLM expands the reachable edit space ~25× beyond
  what has been measured"** rather than "DPLM beats Tranception".
- Report `hit_rate_labeled` AND `labeled_fraction` jointly, not just
  the hit rate.
- Optionally include an "ESMFold-based plausibility check" in the
  supplementary as a sanity check on the generated variants the DMS
  cannot evaluate (currently a stub at `edit_structure_sanity.py`;
  not run).

### 11.3 F7YBW8 calibration outlier

The DMS function prior is poorly calibrated on F7YBW8 (ECE 0.535).
This dominates the pooled test-set ECE (0.106). Honest options:
1. Report per-protein ECE and explicitly flag F7YBW8.
2. Drop F7YBW8 from the calibration claim (but keep it in the hit-rate
   comparison since ranking quality is still good).
3. Add a temperature-scaling baseline fit on val.

### 11.4 ESM-IF1 is a smaller model than ESM-2 650M

ESM-IF1 has 142M params vs ESM-2's 650M — the 4.5× size gap may explain
why structure-conditioning ranks below sequence-only here. A future
revision should add **ProteinMPNN** (~3M params, MIT license, public
checkpoint) as a second structure-conditioned baseline. The adapter
is in `src/phaseagent/structure_baselines.py` and the pipeline is
precompute→cache→lookup; swapping the scoring step is one new function.

### 11.5 Classifier guidance is post-hoc, not per-denoising-step

The DPLM headline uses **guide-by-rerank-after-generation**, not
per-step gradient guidance through DPLM's denoising loop. The standard
classifier guidance for discrete diffusion is per-step (e.g., Plug-and-Play
Discrete Diffusion). A future revision can add per-step guidance once
a differentiable soft-sequence path through the DMS prior is wired
up. The current approximation is the standard fast version.

### 11.6 The DMS prior is pre-trained on 7 proteins

The prior trained on 7 train-split proteins; if the paper grows the
test set, the prior may need retraining on more proteins. The
training pipeline is in `train_editguard_prior` and is split-aware.

## 17. v2 extensions

The v1 EditGuard pipeline (§§4–10) is preserved unchanged. This section
records the v2 extensions added after the v1 freeze, designed to address
the three reviewer concerns most likely to surface:

1. *"Only 3 test proteins — does this generalize?"* → **few-shot regime + OOD family split.**
2. *"Is this useful to the field, or only to ML benchmarkers?"* → **Megascale stability-editing track.**
3. *"What about the F7YBW8 calibration failure?"* → **per-protein conformal abstention.**

Plus one stretch method contribution: **Iterative Twisted SMC on DPLM-650M**.

### 17.1 Few-shot DMS regime

Real protein engineers do not have full DMS datasets — they have ~50–200
hand-measured variants. We added a few-shot evaluation track that
subsamples the train DMS to {50, 100, 200, 500} variants per assay using
three strategies (random / stratified-by-mutation-distance / active
acquisition with UCB), with 10 random seeds per cell.

- Module: [src/phaseagent/few_shot.py](src/phaseagent/few_shot.py)
- Modal entrypoint: `modal_app_v2.py::run_few_shot_sweep`
- Output: `outputs/editguard/few_shot_sweep.csv`
- Active acquisition follows the EVOLVEpro recipe (Jiang et al., *Science*
  2025): RF on chemistry/position features as the surrogate, top-N greedy
  with optional UCB exploration.

### 17.2 OOD family split

The default ProteinGym split is in-distribution by protein family. We added
a leave-one-family-out OOD evaluation: hold out an entire curated family
(fluorescent proteins, toxin-antitoxin, photoreceptor, viral capsid, etc.)
as the test set; train on the rest.

- Module: [src/phaseagent/family_splits.py](src/phaseagent/family_splits.py)
- Modal entrypoint: `modal_app_v2.py::run_ood_family_eval --held-out-family <name>`
- Output: `outputs/editguard/ood_family_metrics.csv`
- Hard guard: `assert_no_family_leakage` runs at the top of every OOD
  benchmark to refuse runs that contain held-out-family rows in train/val.

### 17.3 Megascale stability-editing track

Per the field-mandated multi-mutant evaluation protocol (ThermoMPNN-D
2025; MULTI-evolve critique 2026), Megascale evaluation has three tiers:

- **T1 — single test**: `dataset3_single` test split (ThermoMPNN's
  published split). Pure single-mutant prediction sanity check.
- **T2 — double mutants**: `dataset2` double-mutant subset, k=1 → k=2
  generalization. *Always* reported alongside the additive baseline (sum
  of single-mutant predictions). Headline = Δ(model − additive) on the
  epistasis slice (where |obs − additive| > 0.5 kcal/mol) plus
  stabilizing-pair recall@k.
- **T3 — k≥2 extrapolation**: PTmul-NR. Loaded separately because the
  source is not on Hugging Face.

- Loader: [src/phaseagent/megascale.py](src/phaseagent/megascale.py) — wraps
  `RosettaCommons/MegaScale` HF dataset, normalizes mutation notation,
  rank-norms ΔΔG into [0, 1] per protein.
- Editing tasks: [src/phaseagent/megascale_tasks.py](src/phaseagent/megascale_tasks.py)
  — protect top-quartile single-mutant positions ("don't break what
  works"), forbid liability residues C/M/W/P, three objectives:
  `stabilize`, `destab_avoid`, `stabilize_safe`.
- Additive baseline (the field-required null model):
  [src/phaseagent/additive_baseline.py](src/phaseagent/additive_baseline.py)
- Modal entrypoints: `modal_app_v2.py::download_megascale`,
  `train_prior_on_megascale`, `run_megascale_t2_with_additive`.
- Outputs: `outputs/megascale/prior_singles_metrics.csv`,
  `outputs/megascale/t2_doubles_metrics.csv`.

### 17.4 Per-protein conformal abstention

The v1 C3 result was honest: ECE = 0.035 on GFP, 0.117 on GCN4, 0.535 on
F7YBW8. Per-protein temperature/isotonic scaling fixes most of this; the
remaining gap is handled by selective prediction.

- Per-protein isotonic calibration is now opt-in on `DMSFunctionPrior`
  (`per_protein_calibration=True`); the `fit` method builds one calibrator
  per dataset_id with a global fallback when n_per_protein < 30.
- Conformal layer: [src/phaseagent/conformal.py](src/phaseagent/conformal.py)
  — split-conformal regression with optional Mondrian (per-protein)
  calibration. Returns prediction intervals + a per-protein abstention
  mask. The abstention curve (hit rate as a function of abstention rate)
  is the panel-b figure for Fig. 3.
- Modal entrypoint: `modal_app_v2.py::run_conformal_abstention`.
- Output: `outputs/editguard/conformal_abstention_curve.csv`.

### 17.5 Embedding-based baselines (EVOLVEpro / FLIP2 family)

To respond to the "are RF features the best you can do?" reviewer, we added
three additional baselines:

- **RF on ESM-2 mean-pool embeddings** (the EVOLVEpro winning configuration).
- **LightGBM on ESM-2 mean-pool** (FLIP2-validated competitor to RF).
- **Ridge on one-hot mutation features + zero-shot likelihood** (the
  FLIP2 surprisingly-strong baseline that matches fine-tuned PLMs in the
  small-data regime).

Modules: [src/phaseagent/embeddings.py](src/phaseagent/embeddings.py),

### 17.6 Iterative Twisted SMC on DPLM-650M (stretch method contribution)

Per the SMC research scout (the guided-discrete-diffusion-with-particles
space is crowded but DPLM-650M itself is unclaimed), we added a sampler
combining:

- **Inner loop**: twisted SMC with first-order Taylor approximation for the
  non-differentiable RF-DMS reward (Ou/Pani/Li 2025, smc_ddm).
- **Outer loop**: Reward-Guided Iterative Refinement noise-then-denoise
  evolutionary loop (Uehara et al., ICML 2025).
- **Reward**: the trained `DMSFunctionPrior` (RF on chemistry features).

Module: [src/phaseagent/twisted_smc.py](src/phaseagent/twisted_smc.py). The
algorithm is implemented behind `DPLMBackbone` and `RewardModel` protocols
so the unit tests exercise the control flow with a mock backbone, and the
production GPU run wires in the real DPLM-650M.

**Compute parity for fair comparison.** SMC with K=8 particles × T=128
denoising steps × N_outer=2 outer iterations = 2048 forward passes,
matched against best-of-2048 vanilla DPLM rerank for the headline number.
The full audit reports the curve over total compute (best-of-32 → best-of-4096).

**Mandatory baselines** (per the SMC scout):
- Vanilla DPLM-650M sampling
- Best-of-N RF rerank (existing 0.865)
- β-classifier guidance (DPLM-style; or Schiff/Nisonoff CFG)
- DRAKES (Wang et al., ICLR 2025) — the strongest existing protein
  discrete-diffusion baseline
- SVDD-PM / SVDD-MC (Li/Uehara et al., NeurIPS 2024)
- Reward-Guided Iterative Refinement (Uehara et al., ICML 2025)
